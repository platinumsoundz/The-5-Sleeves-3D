# Chapter 5 — Visual style and rendering language

## Source and authority
**Sources:** `The_5_Sleeves_Anime_Manga_Visual_Style_Guide.txt` (v1.0) and the global production lock in `The_5_Sleeves_Book_One_75_Page_Canva_Visual_Design_Prompt_Pack_v2_0.txt` (v1.0). Treat as visual guidance, not permission to add story facts. The guide names approved Ajaye/model and concept-page references as authoritative, but those image files were not included in this upload. When supplied, the reference images outrank text descriptions for exact appearance.

## Overall art direction
- Full-colour anime/manga graphic novel; not chibi, Western superhero comic style, or photorealism.
- Polished digital illustration, confident anime linework, painterly environmental detail, controlled texture, cinematic light.
- Grounded, serious, human and adventurous with quiet tension. Warmth and humour come from acting and ordinary life, not exaggerated cartoon gags.
- Sequential comic craft: clear reading order, purposeful panel shapes, strong silhouettes, varied shot scale and clean negative space for later lettering.
- Near-future ecological/infrastructure stress should coexist with recognizable everyday life and repaired contemporary surroundings.

## Palette and light
- Clothing/equipment: charcoal, near-black, graphite, deep blue-black.
- Ruined infrastructure and distance: muted steel blue, slate, blue-grey, fog-grey.
- Repair, straps, rubble and salvage: weathered brown, olive, dusty khaki, rust, desaturated earth.
- Sleeve tech: restrained cyan/electric blue reserved for established tech; do not make every object glow.
- Warmth: amber, pale gold and restrained orange for dawn/sunset, lamps, interiors and human moments. Preserve natural skin/hair variation; avoid a uniform grey filter.
- Directional cinematic light and atmospheric depth. Haze softens far structures without erasing them. Tech light can subtly reflect on nearby skin/cloth/metal. Keep highlights selective; avoid universal gloss. Cool exterior / warm interior contrast only when scene supports it.

## Line, shading, materials
- Controlled linework: stronger silhouette contours, finer facial features and seam detail.
- Defined form shading, layered cel shading blended with painterly gradients. Faces expressive and individual, never generic.
- Hair built from readable locks/curls with intentional highlights and shadow masses. Ajaye's curls especially must remain dense and springy, not straight, loose-wave, jet-black, or generic spiky anime hair.
- Distinguish cloth, leather, canvas, rubber, metal, plastic and skin through surface treatment. Show worn edges, seams, patches, folds and material weight. Texture communicates use/age; avoid noise that obscures silhouettes. Characters/action remain the first read.

## World and technology look
- Visual target is circa-2036 recognizable near-future collapse, not a generic distant cyberpunk city. Show inhabited settlements, repaired homes, markets, workshops, clinics/schools, salvaged tools/equipment, familiar roads/vehicles and improvised infrastructure as scene warrants.
- Ruins derive from ordinary contemporary places, degraded or repurposed. Nature reclaims some locations, not every scene as jungle/wasteland. Ordinary animals/life may remain.
- Fog is atmospheric/environmental danger, not automatically a magical wall or universal instant unbreathability; preserve local variation and visibility.
- Towers: monumental, old, purposeful, distinct from everyday ruins; use silhouette, weather and controlled light for scale.
- Devices: damaged continuations of contemporary screens, sensors, cables, power hardware, terminals, modular parts. UI is simple, high-contrast, diegetic, limited and readable. Avoid random interface text/clutter. Technology remains bounded by power, compatibility, permissions, damage and access.

## Composition, camera, acting
- Clear panel borders and stable reading order (left-to-right/top-to-bottom unless a deliberate manga layout is used consistently). One main beat per panel; vary wide establishing, full-body action, medium two-shots, emotional close-ups, object/UI inserts and reactions.
- Establish geography before close-ups; readers must understand where people stand and move. Continuous action preserves screen direction, body orientation, hand positions, light direction and object placement. Avoid repetitive angles unless intentional.
- Gestures, weight distribution, shoulder/hand position, gaze and interpersonal distance carry emotion. Avoid mannequin poses; anatomy, balance and grips must be plausible.
- Use low angles for towers/threats, high angles for vulnerability/geography, over-shoulder for discovery, tight inserts for Sleeve/module details. A front/side/back turnaround must show genuinely different views. Recheck left/right after mirror, crop or camera change.

## Page production template rules (comic only)
The prompt pack requires one complete portrait comic page, target exactly 1080x1536 px, clean outer margin, clear gutters, multi-panel intentional top-to-bottom flow, unless a page explicitly calls for a dominant splash. Verify exported dimensions. It is an ART-only pass: no dialogue, speech balloons, captions, narration, SFX lettering, page numbers, logos, watermarks, readable signs/UI, or invented symbols resembling writing. Leave negative space and add approved lettering later. If Canva cannot create exact dimensions, resize/crop only if no panel/character/action is lost. Inspect every image; text prompts cannot guarantee compliance.

**Do not apply the comic portrait pixel dimensions to the browser game canvas.** The visual style is useful as design language; the game is a procedural 3D whitebox with separate performance needs.

## Game translation status (2026-10-22)
The browser build is still a procedural low-poly whitebox, not a cel-shaded comic render. The first settlement/character pass uses the palette above in-game: charcoal/slate clothing, olive/rust/weathered wood repairs, fog-grey distance, warm amber lamps and low sun, with cyan kept for interface/feedback rather than scenery. It adds a patched training-yard perimeter, workshop, house, market awning, water/solar/filter infrastructure, laundry, trees and distant utility silhouettes, plus Ajaye/Sensei silhouette models with simple placeholder faces. Static geometry and character sections are batched by material to keep draw calls low; there are no shadow maps or post-processing. Character likeness remains unverified until approved model sheets arrive. The opening scene has no story-acquired Cipher Sleeve, Cipher powers, Korb or story system; the temporary `CIPHER SLEEVE · TEST` loadout is a left-arm-only developer visual, and the wilderness route/first-tower silhouette is test presentation, not approved story geography. Comic page dimensions remain comic-only.
