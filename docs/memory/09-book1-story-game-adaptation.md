# Chapter 9 — Book One story reference and game adaptation

**Purpose:** Preserve the useful story, character, world, and continuity detail from the newly supplied Book One prose draft, Script Reference v2.0, 75-page Manga Adaptation Blueprint, and Canva Lettering Prompt Pack v2.0. This is a reference for adapting story material into the browser game; it does not replace the game's current implementation or silently approve draft story content.

## Source status and authority

The supplied files are working/draft materials, not evidence that all dialogue, panel art, or story details are approved canon. The Script Reference explicitly calls its dialogue proposed draft. The Canva pack calls for first-pass invented lettering and owner review; its wording is not approved or verbatim manuscript text. The prose manuscript is a full-prose working draft. The Blueprint declares its own adaptation hierarchy, but that hierarchy is specific to the comic adaptation and does not automatically supersede the project's master canon Bible or direct owner decisions.

Use the existing project canon guardrails first. For story-adaptation questions, distinguish:
- **Stable direction repeated across sources:** discovery, departure, Ajaye/Korb's earned alliance, one locally restored first tower, unresolved wider crisis.
- **Working story detail:** manuscript events, character interactions, travel logistics, and the 15-chapter plan.
- **Proposed dialogue:** all dialogue in Script Reference and lettering pack until owner approval.
- **Open mysteries:** ancient and modern collapse relationship, virus, full Cipher history/access, module roster, Dark spirit, and Kael's artificial Sleeve limits.
- **Game implementation:** do not claim a story feature is playable just because it appears here. Existing game memory identifies the current whitebox training/sparring milestone; consult current code before implementation.

## Book One's narrative shape

Working title: **The Perimeter and the First Tower**. Book function: discovery, departure, first companionship, first tower link. Main arc: Ajaye begins as a capable practical fixer who rushes ahead and tends to treat people as problems or tactical pieces. His growth is not simply increased power: he learns to observe, ask, listen, accept uncertainty, and share agency. Korb begins as a wary, joking survivor; his humour covers fear and loneliness. They become companions through choices and earned trust, not instant friendship.

Book ending direction: Ajaye and Korb activate/stabilise one local tower system with willing cooperation between spirits. The wider Fog and world problems remain. A fragmentary signal points toward the Sunken Coast; Kael escapes/withdraws with evidence that true Sleeves can link. They share a poor meal and a warmer relationship. This is a lead into the next journey, not a global cure or complete explanation.

### Working chapter/page map

The Script Reference maps 15 story chapters to roughly five comic pages each: 1 Before the Fogline (1–5); 2 Edge-Rock (6–10); 3 A House Full of Questions (11–15); 4 The Gate Alarm (16–20); 5 What a Pack Can Carry (21–25); 6 The Old Transit Line (26–30); 7 Korb, Professional Survivor (31–35); 8 Wind Comes With Conditions (36–40); 9 The Roadside Exchange (41–45); 10 Red Tracks (46–50); 11 Crimson Interference (51–55); 12 A Price for Passage (56–60); 13 The Tower's Shadow (61–65); 14 The Guardian That Learns (66–70); 15 The First Link (71–75).

The 75-page Blueprint uses a different detailed beat distribution and explicitly clarifies that the larger 75-chapter outline spans five books; Book One is 15 story chapters adapted into 75 comic pages. Its page map places the discovery on pages 9–12, Korb first on page 33, and first link at pages 73–75. The Canva pack follows that latter numbering. Never assume “chapter number,” “story page,” and “manga page” are interchangeable. Check the actual approved page and production file before editing or generating content.

## Story beats and game-useful material

### Settlement and departure

The opening settlement is a recognisable former commuter suburb adapted for survival, not a generic ruin: patched homes, a school gym used for meetings, supermarket car-park gardens, market awnings, livestock, clinic, repaired water pump, solar panels and mismatched batteries, air-filter fans, shared devices, route warnings, and neighbours with ordinary needs. Life continues—food, jokes, repairs, animals, arguments—alongside scarcity and environmental uncertainty. Fog is a green-grey phenomenon with variable local conditions; it is not universally lethal or uniformly dense.

Ajaye trains with Sensei/father, a disciplined teacher who demonstrates and asks rather than delivering lore lectures. Ajaye is fast and capable but overcommits, interrupts, and learns to watch weight shifts and hear the full problem. Mother expresses care through food, practical checks, supplies, and perceptive questions. Family warmth should coexist with worry. The gate, filters, pump, food, and repair stock make choices consequential.

The manuscript includes uneven, recent crop/water/livestock anomalies: pale/silver-veined or striped produce, thin milk, soft or spotted egg shells, and animals off feed. These are observations, not settled new biology or proof of cause. Characters test water/feed and separate animals rather than claiming certainty. Past mutations reportedly faded or diminished as towers stabilised the planet; whether current changes persist, spread, or disappear remains unknown. Do not turn isolated signs into universal mutation rules.

### Edge-Rock, Cipher, and the drone

On a permitted salvage trip to Edge-Rock, Ajaye finds a buried curved plate etched with five linked circles, opens its compartment, and the dark ancient cuff bonds to his **left** forearm. A short, disorienting vision presents five tower points, one flickering, and the word “CIPHER.” A damaged defence drone wakes. Ajaye survives through observation, terrain, staff/knife skill, and a fleeting imperfect weak-point scan—not mastery or an effortless power. The Sleeve may cause numbness, pain, visual strain, and phone interference; photographs/readouts fail to explain it.

The drone follows/appears at the settlement gate, where the fight damages the gate and consumes scarce repair time/parts. A broken projection links a tower, environmental failure, and spreading Fog but is not a complete historical record. The community has reasonable mixed reactions; knowing Ajaye did not intend the danger does not repair the gate. The council permits investigation under conditions (radio/check-ins, observe before approaching machines); departure is an accountable decision, not a prophecy-driven solo adventure.

### Travel, barter, and technology limits

Ajaye leaves with a finite, physically plausible kit: tested water, food, mask/filter and replacement media/seals, first aid, cord, repair wire, torch, map/notebook, tools/weapons actually established, and trade stock. Check supplies, condition, injuries, time and route before carrying resources forward. Paper maps/logs and waypoint checks complement the Sleeve; the signal is not an omniscient minimap.

The transit-line station market, filter repairers, tested-water station, local mesh, repair guild, road exchanges, and route wardens show a functioning but constrained society. Goods have provenance and costs; people trade work, copper, food, battery charge, filters, repairs, or locally trusted tokens. Consumer technology still exists but is old, damaged, battery-limited, permissioned, and incompatible. A local terminal may require a paid connection, signature check, compatible plug-in/app, or access permission; Cipher does not connect universally. The manuscript's specific market-link installation is a draft detail, not a universal technical rule.

Compact hover-assisted scooters are modest shared transport with stabilisers, batteries, range, deposits, return points, maintenance, and terrain restrictions—not flying cars. Powered travel ends before unsafe gullies; characters continue on foot. The repair guild's detour is earned through work/payment and saves them from an unsafe/closed route. Do not conjure equipment, resupply, credit, or transport for free.

### Korb and Wind

Korb is introduced in a precarious, partly comic scavenging-drone/cable incident. His humour is defensive rather than constant gagging. He is agile and reads people/terrain well, but is not universally competent. His consumer Sleeve predates the collapse and is distinct from Ajaye's ancient Cipher; how commercial and spirit-linked Sleeves relate remains open. The Wind spirit's first uncontrolled reaction is emerald/green and limited; use costs Korb stamina and may cause dizziness, imbalance, or exhaustion. Resting the person does not automatically recharge the device. No effortless flight, unlimited gusts, or instant precision.

The sources agree that Korb's protective choice matters, but describe it differently: the prose has him move to protect a third party, then his damaged consumer Sleeve flares and Wind manifests; the Script Reference says a third person is endangered and his choice triggers the reaction; portions of the Blueprint frame the spirit as choosing him after he protects someone, while page-specific beats vary on exactly who saves whom and when Ajaye/Korb catch one another. Preserve the broad principle—choice to protect, not desire for power—without fixing disputed staging until reconciled against approved outline/pages.

Their provisional partnership is negotiated. Ajaye must stop assigning Korb a tactical role and ask what he can do. Korb contributes improvisation, agility, and social perception; Ajaye offers technical observation, repairs, map/route work, and discipline. They retain separate agency and can disagree. Their later cooperation at the tower should be voluntary, not Ajaye commanding Korb or his spirit.

### Rust, Kael, detour, and tower

Rust is known through red-marked evidence and conflicting local testimony. Its convoys may maintain routes/protect settlements while imposing tolls or extracting supplies; accounts depend on who benefited and who paid. Kael is controlled, severe, and convinced imposed order prevents catastrophic mistakes—not a cartoon villain. He demands Cipher and has incomplete inherited/technical knowledge. His artificial obsidian Sleeve has red-black seams and can force impressive output, but is not equivalent to a true relic; its full mechanics and history are open. Cipher/Kael interference is not a complete explanation. Ajaye's instinct to protect Korb rather than the Sleeve shows changing priorities; Kael remains a threat and learns the true Sleeves may link.

The Sky Tower is ancient, monumental, purposeful and tied to environmental/system stability. Its guardian is a system defender that adapts to repeated tactics. Ajaye's repeated/solo approach fails and endangers Korb. Korb observes a pattern Ajaye missed; Ajaye listens, asks, and they combine limited Cipher scans with limited Wind. The prose describes a ground vent/airflow tactic and a control chamber with five channels; these are useful draft staging, not necessarily required game mechanics.

At the decisive control route, Ajaye chooses Korb's safety over the apparent objective. The spirits cooperate willingly; the first link does not grant full Cipher memory, omniscience, or unrestricted control. The tower improves one local system/area, sends a degraded/fragmentary signal toward the Sunken Coast, and leaves the wider Fog unresolved. Exact visible local effect and whether Cipher speaks immediately are approval-sensitive.

## Canon, draft, and unresolved status

### Repeated direction to preserve
- Ajaye is 16 in the Blueprint; technically gifted, trained, curious, practical and impulsive. Appearance is governed by approved visual sheets, not text-only drafts.
- Ajaye's Cipher Sleeve is green and on his **left arm only**; right arm is normal. Existing memory/direct project decision requires this. Do not mirror or duplicate it.
- Cipher is incomplete, limited and non-conversational before the first tower activation under the existing project rule. Early outputs are scans, symbols, pulses, fragments and interface anomalies.
- Korb's Wind is emerald and costly; spirits are agents with agency, not controls or equipment.
- Ajaye starts without a pet; Jax does not accompany him on Book One departure.
- Tower gains are local; the larger Fog/world crisis remains.
- Comic portrait dimensions, panel counts, Canva and lettering workflow are not Three.js canvas requirements.

### Open or conflicting—do not silently settle
1. **First home return vs gate attack:** The prose has Ajaye return, talk/test the cuff and eat dinner, then the drone reaches the gate. The Script Reference and 75-page map generally have a return/accounting then questions/dinner/horn, but the Canva prompts compress or omit transition detail. The earlier memory reflects this; retain explicit travel/escape and timing when adapting.
2. **Discovery page boundaries:** Blueprint places discovery/bonding/vision/drone wake across manga pages 9–12 and excludes it from chapter 1. The Script Reference's approximate 5-pages-per-chapter map places Edge-Rock in pages 6–10. These are different granularity/plans, not a reason to shift approved pages.
3. **Korb's Wind-awakening staging:** Sources differ on the endangered third party, whether Ajaye or Korb rescues whom, and the sequence of protective choices. Only the broad protective-choice principle is reliable until owner reconciliation.
4. **First Cipher speech:** Existing project canon says first genuine communication follows first tower activation. Script Reference proposes brief speech after activation; the prose ends with a wordless impression and does not establish spoken dialogue. Do not implement or write exact spoken lines as decided canon without approval.
5. **First tower effect:** Local stability is repeated, but exact physical effect is unsettled. Do not promise Fog clearing, a global cure, or a particular gameplay buff without a decision.
6. **Age, heritage, visual descriptions and family names/details:** Blueprint text descriptions remain subordinate to approved character sheets and existing owner decisions. Do not use them to overwrite visual memory.
7. **Timeline and lore:** circa 2036/five years after a modern collapse is a draft framing. The ancient catastrophe is distinct from the modern collapse; causal relation, virus, module count/allocation, Darkness/sixth spirit, Cipher's full history, tower history, Kael's artificial device remain open.
8. **Canon hierarchy:** Blueprint's ordered source hierarchy applies to manga adaptation boundaries; it cannot overrule direct owner decisions or the project's primary canon Bible by implication. Raise exact conflicts for owner resolution.

## Game adaptation guardrails

- Treat Book One as a **candidate narrative/content source**, not a scope expansion of the current whitebox. Current project milestone remains village training and nonlethal sparring per memory chapters 02 and README.
- Translate only approved story elements into game systems. Possible later modules include traversal/exploration, interactable repair/barter, route and inventory tracking, nonlethal combat, enemy pattern adaptation, partner agency, and local tower activation; these are suggestions, not committed features.
- Make mechanics express the story's limits: scans are short/incomplete, environmental hazards vary, resources have weight/source/cost, transport has terrain limits, and powers consume host stamina/device capacity separately.
- Preserve the narrative distinction between an interface cue and a spoken character. Do not give Cipher dialogue, navigation authority, a full map, automatic target solutions, or lore access earlier than approved.
- If adapting the guardian, make adaptation observable and learnable, but do not presume the prose's precise vent trick is canon/gameplay. Cooperation should require timing and mutual agency.
- Do not make the tower a global quest-reward machine. One local improvement and a lead onward are the supported ending direction.
- Story-specific comic rules (1080×1536, 4–7 panels, Canva workflow, lettering) stay in chapter 08 and never dictate browser rendering or Three.js architecture.

## Reference workflow
Before story-facing implementation or content changes: read chapters 01, 02, 05–09; inspect current game files and tests; identify what is implemented vs placeholder; cite the exact story source and status; flag unresolved conflicts; request owner direction when a choice would establish canon. After approval, update the canon/decision log and retain draft provenance. Do not describe a proposal as canon or a game feature as implemented without evidence.
